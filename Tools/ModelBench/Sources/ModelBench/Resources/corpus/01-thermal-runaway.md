title: Thermal Runaway Thresholds in LFP Prismatic Cells
summary: Bench testing of lithium iron phosphate prismatic cells under forced overcharge, recording vent temperatures and propagation behaviour.
---

We subjected forty-eight LFP prismatic cells from the Kestrel-9 production line to forced overcharge at 1.5C until venting. The cells were instrumented with four type-K thermocouples each, bonded to the can wall at the terminal, midpoint, base, and vent port.

%%

Venting occurred at a mean can-wall temperature of 218 degrees Celsius, with a standard deviation of 6.4 degrees across the population. This is substantially higher than the 154 degrees Celsius we recorded for the NMC control group tested under identical conditions in the previous quarter.

%%

Propagation to adjacent cells was observed in only three of the forty-eight trials. In each of those three cases the failed cell had been assembled with the thinner 0.28 millimetre aluminium can rather than the standard 0.4 millimetre stock. We consider can thickness the dominant propagation variable.

%%

The exclusion criteria applied before analysis were: cells with pre-test capacity below 92 percent of nameplate, cells that had been cycled more than 50 times during incoming inspection, and any cell whose thermocouple bond failed during the ramp. Nine cells were excluded on these grounds.

%%

Recommendation: retain the 0.4 millimetre can specification for all Kestrel-9 derivatives, and do not carry the thinner can into the cost-reduction programme. The measured propagation risk does not justify the 40 cents per cell saving.
