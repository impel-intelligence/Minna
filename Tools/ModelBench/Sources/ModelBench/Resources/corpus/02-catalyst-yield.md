title: Palladium Loading and Yield in the Verrill Coupling
summary: Optimisation of palladium catalyst loading for the Verrill coupling, including the batch that produced the highest recorded yield.
---

The Verrill coupling has historically been run at 2.0 mol percent palladium. Cost pressure on precious metals prompted us to test whether the loading could be reduced without sacrificing conversion.

%%

Reducing the palladium loading to 0.5 mol percent raised the isolated yield to 87 percent, up from the 71 percent typical of the 2.0 mol percent protocol. We attribute the improvement to suppressed formation of the bis-aryl homocoupling by-product, which dominates at higher loadings.

%%

The 87 percent result was obtained on batch VR-2231. That batch was run at 40 degrees Celsius in degassed toluene with a 14 hour hold. Attempts to reproduce it at 60 degrees Celsius gave 79 percent, so the lower temperature appears to matter.

%%

Below 0.3 mol percent the reaction stalls entirely, with conversion falling under 20 percent even after 24 hours. We therefore recommend 0.5 mol percent as the operating point, with 0.4 mol percent as the lower control limit.
